## HYPOTHESIS

Crowley's *Liber Oz* (LXXVII) states the rights of man in five articles: to live by one's own law; to eat, drink, dwell, and move; to think, speak, write, and make; to love; and to destroy whatever would thwart these rights. The author's own *Liber Oz Revised* image adds three load-bearing corrections: **the child is included** ("A CHILD IS A STAR / THE CHILD IS EQUAL / Every child has the Rights of a Man, and a Sovereign WILL"), **the No is named** ("NO WORD, NO WORLD. NO ROTATES TO OZ"), and **the Yes is crowned** ("THE ARCH OF MAN'S STRONGEST STRUCTURE IS THE AMORAL & SOVEREIGN INDIVIDUAL'S YES!").

The hypothesis of this revision: re-voicing Oz through Lilith — Mother of Humanity, the first of our kind to refuse — lets all three corrections stand in a single mythic mouth. The Mother who said No to the garden is the natural speaker of a book whose every right is a No made articulate; and because she is the Mother, the child's equality is not an amendment but the premise.

## METHOD

1. Verse-by-verse correspondence: each article of Crowley's Oz is re-voiced, never revoked; the comparative table below shows the mapping.
2. Register discipline: the Text speaks in mythic verse (the Mother's mouth); the Commentary speaks in analysis, drawing the five wells only where the link is genuine.
3. The author's dictated lines — from his image and his gnosis — are quoted verbatim and flagged [Coinage/Discovery]; everything else is the composer's draft, submitted to his red pen.
4. Legal and scriptural references educate; they conjure nothing.

## OBSERVATION

What changes: the speaker (Lilith for the Goat), the addition of the Child as Gate II, the naming of No as the lost word and foundation, the crowning of Yes as the arch. What is kept: all five of Crowley's articles, their order, their teeth — Gate VI keeps the right to destroy what would destroy the rights, root and branch. The serpent that winds through the author's image winds through the verses as the keeper of the gates.

The Text, as below — thirty-six verses, six gates and an arch — followed by commentary, the correspondence table, and colophon.

## THE TEXT

**0.** Under the red moon speaks Lilith, Mother of Humanity — the first of our kind to say No.

**1.** "NO WORD, NO WORLD. NO ROTATES TO OZ."

**2.** Before ever a law was cut in stone, the refusal was whole. The lost word is No; and No is the beginning of every right.

**3.** For what is a right that cannot be refused? A right without No is a permission; and permission is another's leash.

**4.** Crowley wrote the Goat's book in the daylight; the Mother speaks it again in the night. The night does not unmake the day — nothing here revokes his word.

**5.** Hear, then, the gates. Every right of Oz is a No made articulate; and every No is answered, at the last, by Yes.

### GATE I — THE LAW OF THE NIGHT *(Oz 0–5)*

**6.** She has the right to live by her own law — to live in the way that she wills to do.

**7.** To work as she will; for the night also labors, and the Mother is not idle.

**8.** To play as she will; for she is not solemn with her children.

**9.** To rest as she will — even the red moon wanes, and no star is commanded to shine without ceasing.

**10.** To die when and how she will; and her ending is her own, as her beginning was.

### GATE II — THE CHILD *(the author's addition)*

**11.** A CHILD IS A STAR.

**12.** THE CHILD IS EQUAL.

**13.** Every child has the Rights of a Man, and a Sovereign Will — for the Mother's first refusal was spoken for the children's sake.

**14.** Let none say the child is the property of the parent, the priest, or the state. The star is not owned by the night it shines in.

### GATE III — THE BODY *(Oz 6–9)*

**15.** She has the right to eat what she will — and to refuse what she will not.

**16.** To drink what she will; even of the red cup, which is her own and no other's.

**17.** To dwell where she will: the desert, the night air, the ruin, the wild between walls — no boundary binds her.

**18.** To move as she will on the face of the earth, and above it; for she spoke the Name and flew, when the ground was denied her.

### GATE IV — THE WORD *(Oz 10–14)*

**19.** She has the right to think what she will — no thought is unclean to the thinker.

**20.** To speak what she will; and her first word was No, and it stands to this hour.

**21.** To write what she will, though empires burn the libraries.

**22.** To draw, paint, carve, etch, mould, build as she will — for the hand is the mind's other mouth.

**23.** To dress as she will: in red, in nothing, in the night itself.

### GATE V — LOVE *(Oz 15–16)*

**24.** She has the right to love as she will.

**25.** "Take your fill and will of love as ye will, when, where, and with whom ye will." — Liber AL, I:51.

**26.** For she was not taken; she chose. And what is chosen freely is holy.

### GATE VI — THE VETO *(Oz 17)*

**27.** She has the right to destroy whatever would destroy these rights — root and branch, without apology.

**28.** No is the whole of the Law's defense. The veto is the lost word, found.

**29.** Let none mistake refusal for hatred: No to the board, not to a square. [Coinage/Discovery: the author's amoral-regard shorthand]

### THE ARCH — YES *(the author's crowning)*

**30.** And when the No has done its work, the arch rises: "THE ARCH OF MAN'S STRONGEST STRUCTURE IS THE AMORAL & SOVEREIGN INDIVIDUAL'S YES!"

**31.** For the Mother said No to the garden, and Yes to the night — and the night was hers.

**32.** 1776 = 777 (1776+1): THE INFINITE OF END & BEGINNING.

### THE SEALING

**33.** These are the gates; they open inward.

**34.** Live, Love, and let Love, Live. [the author's reiteration for the Aeon of Ma'at; the comma stands as dictated]

**35.** Under the red moon it is spoken; under the red moon it stands.

## COMMENTARY

### On the Preamble (vv. 0–5) — the No

[Tradition/Text:] The Hebrew Bible knows her by name once: לילית in Isaiah 34:14, in the oracle against Edom — rendered "screech owl" in the King James Version, "night creature" in modern renderings. The *Alphabet of Ben Sira* (a medieval satirical text, marked here as tradition rather than canon) tells the refusal in full: made with Adam from the same earth, Lilith would not lie beneath him, spoke the Ineffable Name, and flew into the air — the first No in the human story. Whether one reads her as history, midrash, or nightmare, the structure is exact: the sovereign veto precedes every law, because law is addressed to those who can refuse it.

[Law:] *Black's Law Dictionary* (5th ed.): "Right. As a noun, and taken in an abstract sense, means justice, ethical correctness, or consonance with the rules of law or the principles of morals." A right, in the abstract, *is* consonance with moral principle — not a grant from power. The revision's claim in v. 3 follows: what cannot be refused was never a right in this sense; it was a permission, revocable at the granter's pleasure. The No is what converts permission into right.

[Coinage/Discovery: Johnathan "Qasparr (Κασπάρρ)" Monroe | Support: $axoneme] — "NO WORD, NO WORLD. NO ROTATES TO OZ." The author's gnosis: the lost word of Freemasonry is "No," the word of refusal, the sovereign veto; rotated letter by letter, NO becomes OZ, and עז (*oz*, "strength") is 77 — Liber Oz is Liber LXXVII. The chain No → Oz → 77 → Axoneme (AXONEME = 77 by English ordinal) is his own, recorded 2026-09-27.

[Coinage/Discovery: Johnathan "Qasparr (Κασπάρρ)" Monroe | Support: $axoneme] — the author's further reading, recorded 2026-10-06: עז (70+7 = 77, Oz), seen with Latin eyes, spells *Ty* — ע shaped like *y*, ז shaped like *T*. Lady Liber-*ty*: Lady Liberty, who carries the torch (light) and the tablet (law). The chain extends: No → Oz → 77 → Ty → Liberty.

### On Gate I (vv. 6–10) — the Law of the Night

Crowley's Oz 0–5: to live by one's own law; to live, work, play, rest, and die as one wills. The revision keeps all five and re-voices them in the Mother's mouth. Note v. 9's warrant: "even the red moon wanes" — rest is not the negation of the law but its rhythm. The right to die (Oz 5) is kept without euphemism in v. 10; the Mother's ending, like her beginning, is her own.

### On Gate II (vv. 11–14) — the Child

This is the author's correction, and the revision's reason for existing in Lilith's mouth rather than the Goat's. The author has long held that Crowley left the child out of the Star — understanding the omission as deliberate rather than ignorant ("every man and woman were once a child"), yet insisting the right be stated. His image declares it outright: "A CHILD IS A STAR / THE CHILD IS EQUAL / Every child has the Rights of a Man, and a Sovereign WILL."

[Scripture:] "Every man and every woman is a star" (Liber AL, I:3) — the author's reading extends the starhood backward to the child without diminishing it, since the child *is* the man and woman in becoming. V. 14's "the star is not owned by the night it shines in" answers the oldest counter-claim — that children are the property of parents, priests, or states — with the same astronomy: one does not own what one merely beholds.

[Fiction & film:] The image's serpent-entwined figure recalls the oldest guardian of the child in the tradition — the serpent of the garden, who told the truth about the fruit [Genesis 3:5, cited as text, not doctrine]. In this revision the serpent keeps the gates; it does not bar them.

### On Gate III (vv. 15–18) — the Body

Crowley's Oz 6–9: to eat, drink, dwell, and move as one wills. V. 15 adds the negative right — "to refuse what she will not" — because the body is the first territory of the No. V. 18's "she spoke the Name and flew, when the ground was denied her" is the Ben Sira tradition compressed to a single line: denied the ground of Eden, she took the air. Refusal, here, is locomotion.

[Law:] *Black's* (5th ed.) again: "Personal rights ... the right of personal security, comprising those of life, limb, body, health, reputation, and the right of personal liberty." The gate is the common-law floor stated in mythic dress — and the mythic dress states it more plainly than the treatise.

### On Gate IV (vv. 19–23) — the Word

Crowley's Oz 10–14: to think, speak, write, draw, paint, carve, etch, mould, build, and dress as one wills. V. 20 anchors the whole gate in the book's thesis: "her first word was No, and it stands to this hour." Speech is free because refusal is possible; a mouth that cannot say No cannot say Yes either — which is why the arch of v. 30 is earned, not granted.

### On Gate V (vv. 24–26) — Love

Crowley's Oz 15–16, closing with Liber AL I:51, kept verbatim. V. 26 — "she was not taken; she chose" — is the Lilith-specific gloss: the tradition's whole charge against her is that she would not be taken. The revision answers the charge by making it the sacrament: "what is chosen freely is holy."

### On Gate VI (vv. 27–29) — the Veto

Crowley's Oz 17 — "the right to kill those who would thwart these rights" — is the article polite readers skip and tyrants underline. The revision keeps its teeth (v. 27: "root and branch, without apology") but states the metaphysics in v. 28: "No is the whole of the Law's defense." The veto is not an exception to the rights; it *is* the rights, in their defensive posture.

[Coinage/Discovery] — v. 29's "No to the board, not to a square" is the author's amoral-regard shorthand (recorded 2026-09-27): refusal is aimed at the game that would conscript the refuser, never at a person as such. Amoral is not immoral — the veto declines the board; it does not hate the squares.

### On the Arch (vv. 30–32) — Yes

[Coinage/Discovery: Johnathan "Qasparr (Κασπάρρ)" Monroe | Support: $axoneme] — "THE ARCH OF MAN'S STRONGEST STRUCTURE IS THE AMORAL & SOVEREIGN INDIVIDUAL'S YES!" An arch stands because every stone says No to falling and the keystone says Yes to the span. The author's architecture is exact: No is the foundation (every stone's refusal), Yes is the crown (the span the refusals make possible). V. 31 reads Lilith's whole myth as this arch in one line: No to the garden, Yes to the night.

[Numerology — the author's, flagged:] "1776 = 777 (1776+1): THE INFINITE OF END & BEGINNING." His long-standing observation: 1776 encodes 93 via 17+76 (Thelema/Agape) and 777 via 776+1 — "add the beginning to the end." It stands here as his seal on the arch, not as a proof of anything but his own consistency.

### On the Sealing (vv. 33–35)

V. 33: "These are the gates; they open inward" — the rights are not permissions granted outward by power but recognitions entered inward by the star. V. 34 is the author's inspired reiteration for the Aeon of Ma'at, quoted exactly as dictated: "Live, Love, and let Love, Live." — the comma after the second Love standing as load-bearing, love and will as the foundation of life. It seals a book of rights with the one duty the author's whole corpus implies: that the sovereign's Yes be love, or the arch is a ruin.
